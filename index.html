I'm a computational materials scientist. I build machine-learned interatomic potentials and use them, together with first-principles calculations, to understand how real materials behave: how ions move through battery cathodes, how amorphous solids store and lose energy, and how porous cages flex and bind guests.

I'm currently a postdoctoral researcher in the Computational Modelling group of Prof. Markus Kraft at the University of Cambridge, working on the digital discovery of metal-organic polyhedra. Before that I modelled Li- and Na-ion cathodes with Prof. Saiful Islam at the University of Oxford, as part of the Faraday Institution's CATMAT project, and worked on low-temperature excitations in glasses with Francesco Zamponi at the École normale supérieure in Paris.

I did my PhD in Chemistry at Cambridge with Stephen Elliott and Gábor Csányi, where I fitted one of the first transferable machine-learned potentials for the Ge–Sb–Te phase-change memory materials.

Research

  • Battery cathodesOxford · 2023–2025

    Oxygen redox and voltage fade in Li-rich and Na-ion layered oxides, olivine phosphates, and thin-film conversion cathodes, with experimental partners across CATMAT.

  • Metal-organic polyhedraCambridge · 2026–

    Machine-learned potentials for porous molecular cages for carbon capture, drug delivery and photocatalysis, and how well they capture vibrational properties.

  • Glasses and two-level systemsENS Paris · 2020–2023

    Finding the microscopic tunnelling defects that dominate the low-temperature physics of glasses, with simulation and machine learning.

  • Phase-change memoryCambridge · 2014–2020

    A Gaussian approximation potential for Ge2Sb2Te5, defects and radiation tolerance, and thermal transport in amorphous chalcogenides.
